The Sacred valley of the Incas

All those who are fascinated by the mysterious history and the legacy of the Inca Empire can visit the Sacred Valley, located in the middle of the Andes mountain range.

The Sacred Valley of the Incas snakes through a narrow valley following the Urubamba River. Between the towns of Pisac and Ollantaytambo, it has marvelous landscapes, where its inhabitants include native Quechuan families who conserve many high mountain customs and ancestral rites.

The villages of the Sacred Valley of the Incas, have the fusion of Inca and Spanish architectural style. The wealth of its people is demonstrated by their folklore, crafts, clothing, and way of life, make it a place that leaves unforgettable memories.

Detailed Itinerary

Pisac:
Pisac Market is one of the most famous markets in the Cusco region, drawing many visitors to its famous Sunday market when indigenous Quechua communities from the surrounding highlands come to Pisac to sell their produce.

They set up shop on the main square. Dressed in their colorful clothing, they sit cross-legged with their wares laid out giving the market an authentic feel. It’s definitely worth a visit but be warned this is a tourist hot spot so be prepared for savvy vendors.

The ruins of Pisac are perched on the hills that overlook the little town. On a clear day you have beautiful views over the Inca Terraces and surrounding valley below. The ruins hold a very interesting history and include Temple of the Sun, stone baths, altars, water fontains, and ceremonial platforms.

Ollantaytambo:
Ollantaytambo Archaeological Site built by Incas, is made up of a series of imposing terraces, temples, platforms, paths, and walls. The entire site offers excellent views of the surrounding countryside, the town Ollantaytambo and Incan storehouses or ‘Qollqa’ built on the Pincuylluna Mountain opposite the ruins.

This site is one of the few places that the Spaniards lost a battle during the conquest.

Chincheros:
Chinchero was the place chosen by Inca Tupac Yupanqui to establish his residence. Today Chinchero is interesting for the colonial church built directly upon the walls of the Incas. This Catholic Church is an amazing symbiosis of both Spanish colonial and Incan cultures. Inside it is covered with classical Christian paintings combined with traces of Incas culture and religion. Outside, the Christian cross has carved Incan symbols installed on a staged base symbolizing Pachamamma Goddess (the Mother Earth). Chincheros is also well known for its gorgeous woven products and colorful market. Smaller market than the one in Pisac but offers a variety of beautiful crafts.
